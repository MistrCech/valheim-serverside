# Ward guard

The whole of `[WardGuard]`: what the game's ward protects and where, each rule, what the guard does not cover and how it was tested. The README keeps a summary; the settings are in its Configuration table.


What the game's ward (guard stone, `PrivateArea`) protects, and where that is decided (Valheim 1.0.20, from the game's own code and prefab data). Every check is made in the acting player's own game -- `PrivateArea.CheckAccess` reads the local player -- so the server believes whatever a game tells it: building, taking a piece down with the hammer, opening a chest or a door, changing a sign or a portal's name. Nothing checks the ward when a piece is **hit**: a weapon, an arrow or an explosion goes through `WearNTear.RPC_Damage`, which only makes the ward flash. A player with a sword can take down a warded wall, a chest or the ward itself (1000 health; an axe and a pickaxe do nothing to it, a sword, a mace or a club do), and the PvP switch is no help, it concerns players and not what they build. Smelters, kilns, cooking stations, fireplaces, beds and planted crops have no ward check at all. A ward also protects nothing until its owner has switched it on (E), and a player whose name is on the list of one covering ward may act in all of them.

With `[WardGuard] Enabled` the server decides, for every game that talks to it, by the game's own rule (`PrivateArea.CheckAccess`): a player may act where no **enabled** ward covers the place (the ward's radius, 32 m, measured sideways), or where one of the covering wards has them on its list -- its owner, or a player who put their name on it with E. Every routed RPC a client sends for an object that is not its own passes `ZRoutedRpc.RPC_RoutedRPC` on the server; it is looked at there, before anything is done with it, and judged by the player the connection belongs to. This mod's core patch makes the server the owner of what stands near players (vanilla gives it to a player's game), so that holds for the builds of others; only an object a player's own game owns -- one it has just placed, while it stays in the area -- handles its requests in that game without the server, and such an object is the player's own.

- **Structures** (`Structures`): `RPC_Damage`, `RPC_Remove` and `RPC_Repair` for a build piece (anything with a creator, and the ward itself) in a ward, from a player who may not act there, are dropped; the ward flashes for everyone near, as it does for a hit, and the player is told. A hit with no attacker (fire, a falling object) or by a creature that the sender's game owns (a raid, a lured monster) is not a player's and is not touched: monsters do what they do in the game. Admins are not held to a ward (`ExemptAdmins`; for structures and interactions only, not for spoofing and destroy requests). Ships and carts are pieces: hitting one inside a ward is judged, steering it is not.
- **Outside wards** (`OutsideWards`, Off by default): a player's build is its builder's. Where no enabled ward covers the place, or where the covering wards do not let its builder act either (a ward someone else put up beside it), `RPC_Damage` and `RPC_Remove` for it (anything with a creator, and a ward that is switched off), and the uses the Interactions rule lists -- opening a chest or a door, a stand, taking from a smelter, kiln or cooking station, switching a fire off, claiming a bed, picking a crop --, are dropped unless the sender built it, is on the list of one of the builder's wards (switched on or not: the people the builder lets into their base) or is an admin, and the player is told (`OutsideWardsMessage`); a chest answers no. Repairing and putting things in stay open to everyone, and a hit with no attacker or by a creature the sender's game owns is let through, as under Structures. Where the builder's own ward, or one they are on, covers the place, that ward's list decides. So a ward gives its owner no rights over the builds of people who are not on its list: nobody can put up a ward beside someone's base and take it apart -- and a ward's owner cannot take a non-member's pieces down inside their own ward either; the builder (with `OwnPieces`) or an admin can. It is meant for a server where nobody should break or empty what others built, ward or not: a base that grew past its ward, a ship, a portal far from home. A piece with no creator -- what the world itself placed, a village, a dungeon -- is not a player's build and stays open.
- **Crops** carry their planter: a grown crop is a new object with no creator, so the server writes the planter of the sapling it grew from onto it, matching the crop that appears with the sapling that goes (within a minute and within the plant's reach, whichever game grows it). A crop planted before 1.31.0, or a wild one, has no planter and stays open outside wards.
- **Own pieces** (`OwnPieces`, false by default): a player may always hit, take down, repair and use what they built, also inside a ward they are not on -- in the game a ward covers whatever stands in its radius, a neighbour's build too.
- **Tombstones** (`Tombstones`, Off by default): `RPC_RequestOpen`, `RPC_RequestTakeAll` and `RPC_RequestStack` for a tombstone are refused to anyone but the dead player (the tombstone's owner, which the game writes when it drops it), the players on the list of one of their wards and admins; the tombstone answers no, as it does when it is in use, and the player is told (`TombstonesMessage`).
- **Interactions** (`Interactions`): what the game's client already refuses (chests and doors with the guard stone flag -- the iron, wood, black metal and grausten chests, barrels and wardrobes, 20 kinds of door --, item and armour stands, fermenters, beehives, sap collectors, incinerators, writing on the map table) is refused by the server too, and so is what has no check: taking the product out of a smelter, kiln or cooking station, switching a fireplace off, claiming a bed, picking planted crops. A chest asked to open answers the player no, as it does when it is in use. Personal chests (`piece_chest_private`) are the creator's and need no ward. Putting something in -- ore, fuel, food, ammo -- is left alone: it is a gift, and the player's game has already taken the item out of their inventory when it asks, so a refusal would only destroy it. Traps are left alone too: the same request is sent when something steps on one.
- **Spoofing** (`Spoofing`): the sender id in a routed packet is the client's own claim. A packet whose id is not the connection's is dropped; the game's own client always sends its own. A ward's `ToggleEnabled` and `TogglePermitted` take the player's id as a parameter: a modified game could switch off someone's ward by sending its owner's id, or put itself on the list -- both are dropped when the id is not the sender's own.
- **Destroy requests** (`DestroyRequests`): the game asks the server to destroy objects its own game owns, so a request for objects that another game or the server owns is what a modified game sends. But the game also claims an object and destroys it in the same frame (the hammer on a piece that is not a `WearNTear`, picking up, harvesting), and the claim can reach the server after the request, so an honest client shows up in the count too. LogOnly (the default) counts them and writes a summary (five minutes after the first, or when the player leaves); On takes them out of the request. Leave it in LogOnly until the log shows what the honest clients do.

- **Limit on switched-on wards** (`MaxActiveWards`, 2; 0: none): a ward is switched on by its creator pressing E on it (`ToggleEnabled`; the game does nothing for anyone else). A creator who already has that many wards on is refused -- the request is dropped and they are told in the middle of the screen -- until they switch one off or take it down. A ward that a player has just placed is owned by that player's game, which handles the key press itself without asking the server, so the limit is also kept on what is: every five seconds the server counts the wards that are on and switches off the newest of a player who has too many (taking the ward over and writing `enabled` off), and tells them. Building more is not held back. The wards that are on when the server starts, or when the limit is changed, stay on. A player is held to it while they are in the game, and every ward above the limit goes off, the newest first. Admins are not held to it. Without a limit the world fills with wards, and under Base decay every ward is a base that is kept alive; the limit asks no second kind of ward and no mod on the client.

The six rules of 1.33.0 (all `Off` by default; `Off`, `LogOnly` or `On`, except `DestroyLog`, a switch; admins are exempt from all but `DestroyLog`, which only writes):

- **Destroy log** (`DestroyLog`): a line in the guard log (`ward guard: destroyed: ...`) every time a player's build is destroyed by someone other than its builder, whatever the other rules say: whose piece it was, which, where, and who did it -- a player; a creature and the player it was after; the kind of hit and the game that sent it (a hit with no attacker); "took it down" (the hammer, `RPC_Remove`); "collapsed or removed by the server" (a piece that lost its support has no one). One line per destroyer and builder a minute, with a count and up to eight prefab names. A request to destroy what another game or the server owns (`DestroyRequests`) is written too. Nothing is stopped.
- **Claims** (`Claims`): a game takes over an object by telling the server it owns it now, in the data it sends (`RPC_ZDOData`: the owner field of the packet). A destroy request has no owner check, so taking an object over is the first step of destroying it, or of rewriting it. A claim is when the object is not new (it has a creator), has an owner who is not the sender, the sender is not handing it on, and the sender may not act on it by the rules above. On: the owner stays where it is, the data in the packet is not read, and when the packet is done the server raises both revisions so that every game, the sender's too, gets the server's copy again. LogOnly: the line, and it is let through. An object nobody owns, a new object and the player's own are left alone.
- **Indirect hits** (`IndirectHits`): `Structures` and `OutsideWards` let through a hit that is not a player's own: one with no attacker (fire, a falling object, a catapult, a cart, a boat, a felled tree) or whose attacker is a creature the sender's game owns. A modified game can send any hit as such. On: the hit counts as the sender's own and is refused where the rules would refuse the sender's hand; LogOnly: written with the kind of hit, and let through. A creature the server runs, fire and a falling object with no player behind them are not touched (the guard cannot know whose they are).
- **Tamed animals** (`TamedAnimals`): a tamed animal standing inside an enabled ward is held to the ward's list: a player not on it cannot hit it, give it orders (`Command`), name it (`SetName`), put a saddle on it or take it off, or ride it (`RequestControl`). Admins are exempt.
- **Vehicles** (`Vehicles`): a ship or a cart somebody built may be used only by its builder, the players on the list of one of the builder's wards (switched on or not) and admins, anywhere: taking the helm, steering, owning a cart (`RPC_RequestOwn`), opening a ship's chest, and a catapult's loading, firing and legs. On refuses (the helm and the cart answer no) and the server hands such a ship to itself instead of to the player. A shot sent to a catapult the server owns would be fired by the server itself, out of the guard's sight, so it is dropped for anyone but an admin whenever `Vehicles` or `IndirectHits` is not Off (the stricter decides).
- **Terrain** (`Terrain`, with `TerrainMargin` 2 m): the ground is changed by `RPC_ApplyOperation` on the terrain's own object (the terrain compiler of the zone: a position, a rotation, and the tool, a hash that names a `TerrainOp`), and nothing in the game looks at a ward. A player's operation is dropped (On) or written (LogOnly) when (a) it reaches into an enabled ward whose list the player is not on (the ward's radius plus the operation's; needs `Structures`), or (b) it comes within `TerrainMargin` of the operation's own radius of a build of a player whose side the player is not on (digging beside a wall undermines it; needs `OutsideWards`). The builder (with `OwnPieces`) and admins are exempt.
  The operation reaches that check only when the server owns the compiler, and only the server hands out ownership. A player's game owns a compiler in exactly one case: it created it (`Heightmap.GetAndCreateTerrainCompiler`, the first dig in a zone nobody dug before). Then every operation of that player in that zone runs in the game itself and the server only gets the result (`s_TCData`). So the server keeps the compilers: **a compiler that a player's game creates** (a new object in `RPC_ZDOData`, found to be a terrain compiler once its data is read, whoever the packet names as its owner) is judged at once. If the zone already has a compiler on the server it is a duplicate -- the game removes duplicates by keeping the one with the most operations, so a forged one with a high count would replace a base's ground in every game -- and under On it is destroyed. Otherwise its data is read: the header (version, operations, the point and radius of the last operation), whose last operation is judged by the two tests above, and the heights, every place where the data changes the height of the ground judged the same way in cells of 2 m (a forged compiler can lower a base's ground and name a last operation far from it; the paint is only colour and is not judged); refused under On means destroyed too, and so does data that cannot be read. **Whatever is kept is taken over by the server at once**, so the player's next operation goes to the server, to the check. And **no player's game takes a compiler back**: a game that says it owns an existing compiler is refused the way `Claims` On refuses (the owner stays the server, the data is not read), whatever `Claims` is set to; this also covers the packet the player's game sends before it learns that the server took the compiler. A game that sends the data of an existing compiler it does not own while naming the server or someone else as its owner (no take-over, so `Claims` would not see it) is refused the same way: a compiler's data is written by its owner only. LogOnly writes all of it and lets it be (the take-over line at most once a minute per player). An honest game can draw a line once in a while: the one packet it sends before it learns that the server took its new compiler, and a second compiler from a game that dug before the zone's existing one reached it (under On that dig is undone). An admin's game is exempt. Nothing of this runs with `Terrain` Off.

Every refusal is written to `<world>.guard.log` (the character guard's and the item ledger's file): who, what, where, whose ward (outside wards: whose build, by name and player id, and whose ward is there; tombstones: whose), at most once a minute per player, kind and ward (outside wards and tombstones: per player and builder or dead player), with how many there were since the last line. With LogOnly the line ends "LogOnly: let through" and nothing is stopped; set `Structures`, `Interactions` and `Spoofing` to LogOnly first to see what the rules would do (the limit on switched-on wards has no LogOnly: it is on at 2 as soon as the guard is). `Enabled` needs a restart to switch on; switching it off takes effect at once.

What it does not do: ground items, tombstones without `Tombstones` (anyone may open one, as in the game), crops planted before 1.31.0 outside wards, steering a ship or pulling a cart, the text of a sign (written straight into its data by the player's game), the paint inside the data of a terrain compiler a game sends, and fire and falling or colliding objects (they have no attacker; Fire control limits fire) -- so under OutsideWards a fire arrow still sets a wooden build alight. It cannot stop a game that takes ownership of an object without asking its owner (a boss trophy used on a turret does) and then hits it itself, nor a piece that is out of every ward's radius from carrying warded ones (taking down the pillar takes down what stands on it). And it cannot tell a modified game from an honest one, only refuse what an honest one never sends: a modified game can write an object's data directly, name another player's character as its own (the server takes the identity from the client's character data) or name a creature as the attacker of a hit.

Tested on a copy of the live world with three simulated players (a ward's owner, a stranger and a friend put on the ward's list) sending each request as a client does, with the guard off, On, LogOnly and with `DestroyRequests` On, and with a real, unmodified Valheim 1.0.20 client hitting a warded wall: off the list its hits were refused ("tried to hit woodwall ... refused") and the wall stayed untouched, on the list the wall was hit as in the game, and with the guard off it came down; the owner, a listed friend, creatures of the sender's game and admins were never refused. Finding the wards among 349 000 objects takes 6.5 ms every 5 seconds. The full run is in [docs/TESTS.md](https://github.com/MistrCech/valheim-serverside/blob/main/docs/TESTS.md).
