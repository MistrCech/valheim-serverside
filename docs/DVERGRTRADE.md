# Dvergr trade in detail

Part of the README's Dvergr trade section, moved here for the size of the README (Thunderstore takes at most 100,000 characters of it).

## The stands

Each of eight locations -- the three Mistlands guard towers (`Mistlands_GuardTower1_new` to `3_new`), the lighthouse (`Mistlands_Lighthouse1_new`), the two excavations (`Mistlands_Excavation1`, `2`), the harbour (`Mistlands_Harbour1`) and the Ashlands' charred tower (`CharredTowerRuins1_dvergr`) -- holds exactly one `dverger_guardstone`, the dvergr's own ward (radius 32 m, the camp inside it). When a player comes within `StandRange` of one, the server puts up a stand beside it: a dvergr chest (`TreasureChest_dvergrtown`, the camp's own kind, which anyone may open even inside an enabled dvergr ward) and a sign with the title and one line per offer. The spot is found with physics, on the server, once the camp is really loaded (the ward and every object within 12 m of it exist, so nothing is built into a wall that is not there yet): level ground near the ward's height at 2.5 to 5 m, free of anything, the chest facing away from the ward and the sign beside it. Both carry a marker in their data (`sgg_trade`: 1 chest, 2 sign), by which they are found again after a restart. A chest or sign someone has destroyed is put up again after ten minutes; a camp with no free spot is logged once and tried again in ten minutes. The chest never holds the camp's loot and Resource Regrowth's treasure chests never restock it (`s_cheated` = 1: Regrowth leaves any chest whose `s_cheated` is not 0, while the game marks what an object drops as cheated only at 2, so the wood of a broken chest is clean). The sign's text is rewritten whenever it differs (the offers changed, or someone wrote on it while the ward was off). The sign is not translated for each player: it carries the names in `SignLanguage` (the game's own words in that language, read from its localization files; the server's own language stays as it is), or in the server's language; the messages carry item tokens, which every player's game shows in their own.

## The trade

Put the price in, close the chest. A chest that has been opened stays with the player's game, so the server takes it back every second once it is closed (the game marks a chest in use while it is open), waits a moment if it was just opened (the game's "stack all" and "take all" hand a chest over without marking it, and the player's game writes into it a moment later), and weighs what is in it a second later (a player's game that still owns the chest can grant itself an open just as the server takes it back; the server sees that by the owner revision going down, weighs nothing and gives the chest back). For 2.5 seconds after the server has written the chest, opening it answers "in use": a game loads a chest's contents once a second and never while it is open, so one opened earlier would show the old contents and save them back. Offers are paid in the order of the list, each as many times as it is paid in full; the price items are counted by prefab name, whatever their quality. If the goods do not fit, that trade is undone, no item offer after it is traded (an offer of the ward still is) and the player is told. The goods are new items of quality 1 at the world's level. Items the server leaves in the chest are booked correctly by the item ledger when a player takes them out. Each trade is logged: who, where, what was paid and what was got.

A camp sells the goods of its land for the trophies of its land: `Offers` is the list of the Mistlands, `OffersAshlands` that of the charred tower (empty, or not one offer in it valid: `Offers`, and the log names what was skipped). Which is which goes by the land the game placed the camp's location for, not by the ground under the ward, which can be a neighbour's at the edge of a biome. What a camp's list does not take stays in the chest untouched.

## The ward

`Ward:<hours>` as the reward switches the camp's ward off for that long from now (a second payment adds to what is left; at most 7 days from now: an offer whose hours would go past that is not taken, its trophies stay in the chest and the player is told) and calms every dvergr within twice the ward's radius that was angry: nothing in the game calms an angry dvergr again. The time is kept in the ward's data (`sgg_ward_off_until`, server time), so it survives a restart; like the game's own timers it stands still while nobody is online and jumps when the players sleep. The game switches such a ward on whenever its area is made, so the server keeps it off every two seconds and straight after it is made, and switches it on when the time is up. A disabled ward also stops the game's other cause of anger -- damaging something inside it -- on its own. What remains is picking what lies in the camp (the dvergr lanterns and steins are "theft"); while the ward is off that is not passed on to the dvergr. Hitting a dvergr still angers them; paying again calms them. The ward itself takes no damage while it is off (it would pass no anger on, and a camp without its ward has no stand). The players within `StandRange` are told when it goes off and on.

## The sign

`SignTitle` is the first line of the sign; one line per offer follows, each kept whole in `<nobr>`, and `<size=SignTextSize%>` is around the text. The game sizes a sign's text itself to fill the board (auto size from 1 to 8 on a box of 18.3 x 8.6 at scale 0.05, i.e. 0.91 x 0.43 m), so the percentage changes the board only where the text would need a size under 1 times it: 45 leaves room for a text some twice as long as the Czech one of 24. 10. 2026 (which needs 1.08). The hover text keeps this tag (the game strips tags but not one with `%`), so the percentage is also the size of the text a player reads when pointing at the sign: e.g. 70 makes that larger. Rich text works in the title, and a colour opened at its head and not closed colours the whole sign: the game writes a sign in dark brown (#1E1A12), which hardly shows on the dvergr's dark board in the Mistlands, so e.g. `<color=#F2E6C8>Dvergr trade: ...` makes it light.
