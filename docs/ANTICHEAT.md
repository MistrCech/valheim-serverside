# Anti-cheat: paid planting, the stored world, what it cannot see

Parts of the README's Anti-cheat section, moved here for the size of the README (Thunderstore takes at most 100,000 characters of it).

## Paid planting

PlantEasily, MassFarming, ComfyMassFarming and CropUtils plant a row or a grid of crops with one press. With `PaidPlanting = true` (off by default; `Rate` must not be `Off`) a group of plants that `Rate` would take is let through when the player's own game paid for it as the game does. A plant costs a seed, a little wear on the cultivator, and stamina (5 for a press, down to half with the Farming skill and to 65 % of that in a harvester's dress and hat: 1.625 at the least, times the world's stamina rate). The server sees one of the three: the stamina and the item in the hand are in the player's character object, which the game updates in every physics step in which it changed and sends with everything else. For a group it checks that the cultivator was in the hand and that the stamina went down -- the sum of its falls from the moment the first plant arrived to half a second after the last -- by `PlantStamina` (1.3) for every plant. The plants the stamina paid for stay, in the order they came; the rest are taken away and the player is kicked, as under `Rate`. Once a group is a burst it goes on while its plants arrive less than a second after each other (a hitch of the player's game does not split a grid, and what the next group pays is never counted for this one). A character whose stamina ran out inside the group is as paid as it could be (PlantEasily plants the rest of the grid without looking at the stamina); a character that another game has written to, and an admin, are not held to it. Each group is written once to `<world>.guard.log` with what the server saw, and `anticheat` counts the groups and the plants let through.

By the code of the mods as of October 2026: PlantEasily 2.3.0 charges the game's own price for each plant in the frame of the press (unless its `UseStamina` is off); MassFarming 1.13.0 charges 5 for each extra and stops when the stamina is out (unless `IgnoreStamina` is on); ComfyMassFarming 1.6.0 charges (5 plus the dress's modifier) x 1.77 and has no switch; CropUtils 2.1.1 charges a twentieth (`StaminaDiscountConfig` 20, which divides the wear too) and so pays for a few plants only until its discount is 1. What the server cannot see: why the stamina fell (sprinting or dodging at that moment is credited to the plants, at most what the character had and 15 more), the seeds and the wear of the tool (a mod with only the wear switched off passes), and what a game that forges the values writes. The first plant of a press is paid by the game itself, which pays for one to three plants at the full price, so a group of up to three with free extras cannot be told from a paid one, and over a stretch of presses a mod with free extras gets no more than two to four plants for each press the game charged.

## The stored world

The console command `anticheat scan` looks through everything in the world with the rules that do not need a player: what no tool of the game makes (`NotBuildable`) and objects of a kind the game does not have, dropped items and chests with more in a stack than an item can have, pieces in the wrong biome or in a dungeon, plants put down in groups (and says who made it). It does not look for field overrides, the cheating mark, forged ids, pieces in wards or far from their player: those need the player. `anticheat revert` takes away only what no tool of the game makes (if `NotBuildable` is `On`) and dropped items above their maximum (if `Items` is `On`; a dropped item has no owner on it, so they go whoever dropped them), and never the contents of a chest, a piece in the wrong biome or in a dungeon (the world may have been built under another rule then), an object the game does not have, or a piece of an admin; a piece whose maker the character guard does not know is left alone unless `anticheat revert all` is said (the creator is what the player's game wrote, and without a record there is no telling an admin's from a stranger's). It ignores `Action`, it cannot be undone, and `scan` first shows exactly what `revert` would take. `anticheat` alone says what has been found since the start. On a copy of the live world this was written for, a scan of 600,000 objects takes some tens of milliseconds and found exactly one thing no tool of the game makes -- a thistle -- and no unknown object, nothing in the wrong biome, no stack above its maximum.

## The combat rules (1.33.0)

`Unhurt`, `Stamina`, `Damage` and `Skills` (all `Off` by default; `Off`, `LogOnly` or `On`), and the weapon part of `Items`. A player's game keeps its own health, stamina, skills and damage, and the server is never asked; what it does see is what that game writes to the character object (health, stamina, the item in each hand and its quality, whether it blocks or dodges, whether it is dead), the hits the game sends to what it hit (a routed `RPC_Damage` with the damage, the skill and its level, the quality of the weapon) and the hits that the server's own creatures land on the character (`Character.Damage`). These rules read the traces. There is no object to take away: with `Action = Kick` the player is kicked (`Unhurt` and `Stamina` warn first, see `SoftStrikes`), otherwise the finding is written, and `Damage` On also drops the hit. Admins are exempt. A player's character is judged only when it was made by the player's own game and no other game has written to it. Every finding is followed by an `evidence:` line in `<world>.guard.log` with the last eight hits taken or dealt (`Unhurt`: the raw damage, the damage the armour should have left, the health before and after, blocking and dodging; the others: the target, skill, level, quality, damage and stamina of each hit), to check a `LogOnly` finding by. Nothing runs while the four rules and `Items` are `Off`.

The numbers the rules use are the game's own, worked out once from `ObjectDB` when they are first needed and logged ("Anti-cheat: combat bounds from the game", "Anti-cheat: Damage bound"). On the server this was written for (Valheim 1.0.20, world level 0):

- **Unhurt.** God mode clamps a lethal hit to exactly 1.0 health, which an honest character never ends at: two such hits in ten minutes are a finding. Damage immunity leaves the health where it was after hits that nothing could have absorbed: `UnhurtHits` (8) hits in a row from creatures, not blocked and not dodged, each worth more after the armour of what the character wears (each worn piece at its best quality, plus 40 for the status effects that add armour, a quarter over) than the most a protection shield absorbs (700, the largest `SE_Shield` the game has, at skill 100) and not lowering the health, judged on the first data the player's game sends after the hit (a hit with no fresh data after 6 s is dropped from the count). A player who is never hit is never seen.
- **Stamina.** An attack pays its price when it starts. `StaminaAttacks` (12) melee attacks in 20 seconds with a weapon that costs 4 stamina or more (the cheaper of the primary attack and a secondary attack with an animation of its own; a bow, a spell and fists are not counted, and a second hit of one swing less than a quarter of a second after the first counts once) while the stamina the game reports stays within 1 of itself and above 0 are a finding, if the character keeps sending data (its revision rose by 10 or more). The stamina is read twice: at each hit, and every half second while the character has attacked in the last 20 seconds. An honest character whose stamina is back at the top at every attack gives the same value at every hit, but the game waits a second before it regenerates after an attack, so between two swings the character shows "top minus cost" for at least that second; the finding needs the samples between the swings (at least three taken since the first attack of the window) to stay within 1 as well, and says both ranges.
- **Damage.** `MaxDamage` 0 (the default) is automatic: the strongest hit a weapon a player can make sends, with every multiplier at its largest, times 1.5. A hit is worked out as the weapon's damage at its top quality (`m_damages + m_damagesPerLevel x (maxQuality - 1)`), plus what the world level adds to a weapon (`ItemData.GetDamage`: `IncreaseEqually(worldLevel x 120, ...)`; the game spreads it over the damage types and the chop part grows on top, so the server asks the game's own method for the total, nothing at world level 0), times the attack's multipliers (the special attack, the last hit of a chain, the damage missing health gives); a bow counts with the strongest arrow. Status effects then work as in `SE_Stats.ModifyAttack`: each damage type is raised by the largest percentage any effect gives that type (`m_percentigeDamageModifiers`; on game 1.0.20 blunt and pierce +20%, slash, fire, frost, lightning, poison and spirit +10%, chop and pickaxe +60% from the Elder's power, which only fells trees and breaks rocks faster), and the whole hit is multiplied by the largest `m_damageModifier` (1.25). That is the strongest hit: 1378 (THSwordGold_FrostFire, quality 4, 334 x 3 for its special attack = 1002, 1102 with the percentages of its slash and frost), and the bound is 2067. Only items with a recipe count (what creatures use and what admins hand out has none). The butcher knife (`m_tamedOnly`, 1000 x 3) works on tamed animals only and has a bound of its own, 5625 by the same formula: it is used when the target is tamed and the knife is in the character's hand, and the bound of everything else does not count it. A positive `MaxDamage` is used as it is for every target but that one (a tamed animal and the knife), where the knife's own bound stays. If the bounds cannot be worked out, an automatic `Damage` judges nothing. The hit is the one the player's game sends: a cheat that stays under the bound it cannot see.
- **Skills.** Tracked are Swords, Knives, Clubs, Polearms, Spears, Axes, Bows, Crossbows, Unarmed, Pickaxes, WoodCutting and ElementalMagic. Per character the server keeps (`<world>.skills.txt` next to the world, saved with it every five minutes and when the server stops) the first level it saw of each skill and the experience of the hits it has seen since (every hit counts, which over-counts a swing that hits several targets; the skill's step and the world's skill gain are included); the level a hit carries, less the most that any status effect adds to that skill (Clubs +20, Spears +20, Bows +50, Unarmed +15, ElementalMagic +35, the others +0), must cost at most `SkillGainFactor` (3) times that experience plus `SkillAllowance` (80); the experience from level 0 to level L is the sum of `L^1.5 x 0.5 + 0.5`, so level 10 costs about 76 and level 20 about 330. A new, fresh character (the item ledger's rule, with its `GraceHours`) starts at 0 and is judged exactly; any other character cannot be judged on its first level (it may come from another world, or have been played before the server counted) and is judged only on its growth after it, and a character that brings its skills from another world looks like a cheat if it was opened as a fresh one (use `LogOnly` if you let those in).
- **Items.** A melee hit that carries a weapon quality (`m_itemLevel`) above the quality of the weapon in the character's hand is a finding of `Items`.

What these rules do not see: a damage cheat under the bound; god mode on a player no creature ever hits (and PvP hits, which are not looked at); stamina cheats on a bow or a spell, and cheats that lower the cost a little; experience the server does not count (crafting, building and running raise other skills, which are not tracked); a cheat careful to look like a player.

## What it cannot see, and how it can be got round

Anything the game does inside the player's own game -- how fast a character runs, how hard a weapon hits, what a tool or a recipe costs, how much can be carried, how long a pickaxe reaches (the stamina of a character is in the character object, and `PaidPlanting` reads it for planting, nothing else). A cheat that is careful -- single pieces, within reach, on cultivated ground, with a mod that only turns them -- cannot be told from a player who does not use it. A player's inventory is not on the server, so nothing here can say that a player planted seeds they did not have (`PaidPlanting` checks the stamina a planting takes, not the seeds). An object is judged once, 1.2 s after it arrived: a game that sends an innocent object and rewrites it later is not seen again, and an object whose data never comes is counted and dropped. A game decides what character it calls its own; the rules that read the character (`Fly`, `Teleport`, `Speed`, `Creator`, `Distance`, `Identity`) trust it as far as it is checked (it must be made by the connection's own game), and a game that has none is held to that for pieces. The server accepts a write to any object from any game that sends a newer revision -- the vanilla server does -- so a modified game can still change another player's objects; the anti-cheat acts on a game that writes to a character, and leaves a character that has been written to out of its rules. Thousands of objects at once are not all looked at (`Flood`). And a kick is not a ban.
