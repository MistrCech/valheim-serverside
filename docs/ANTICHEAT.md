# Anti-cheat: paid planting, the stored world, what it cannot see

Parts of the README's Anti-cheat section, moved here for the size of the README (Thunderstore takes at most 100,000 characters of it).

## Paid planting

PlantEasily, MassFarming, ComfyMassFarming and CropUtils plant a row or a grid of crops with one press. With `PaidPlanting = true` (off by default; `Rate` must not be `Off`) a group of plants that `Rate` would take is let through when the player's own game paid for it as the game does. A plant costs a seed, a little wear on the cultivator, and stamina (5 for a press, down to half with the Farming skill and to 65 % of that in a harvester's dress and hat: 1.625 at the least, times the world's stamina rate). The server sees one of the three: the stamina and the item in the hand are in the player's character object, which the game updates in every physics step in which it changed and sends with everything else. For a group it checks that the cultivator was in the hand and that the stamina went down -- the sum of its falls from the moment the first plant arrived to half a second after the last -- by `PlantStamina` (1.3) for every plant. The plants the stamina paid for stay, in the order they came; the rest are taken away and the player is kicked, as under `Rate`. Once a group is a burst it goes on while its plants arrive less than a second after each other (a hitch of the player's game does not split a grid, and what the next group pays is never counted for this one). A character whose stamina ran out inside the group is as paid as it could be (PlantEasily plants the rest of the grid without looking at the stamina); a character that another game has written to, and an admin, are not held to it. Each group is written once to `<world>.guard.log` with what the server saw, and `anticheat` counts the groups and the plants let through.

By the code of the mods as of October 2026: PlantEasily 2.3.0 charges the game's own price for each plant in the frame of the press (unless its `UseStamina` is off); MassFarming 1.13.0 charges 5 for each extra and stops when the stamina is out (unless `IgnoreStamina` is on); ComfyMassFarming 1.6.0 charges (5 plus the dress's modifier) x 1.77 and has no switch; CropUtils 2.1.1 charges a twentieth (`StaminaDiscountConfig` 20, which divides the wear too) and so pays for a few plants only until its discount is 1. What the server cannot see: why the stamina fell (sprinting or dodging at that moment is credited to the plants, at most what the character had and 15 more), the seeds and the wear of the tool (a mod with only the wear switched off passes), and what a game that forges the values writes. The first plant of a press is paid by the game itself, which pays for one to three plants at the full price, so a group of up to three with free extras cannot be told from a paid one, and over a stretch of presses a mod with free extras gets no more than two to four plants for each press the game charged.

## The stored world

The console command `anticheat scan` looks through everything in the world with the rules that do not need a player: what no tool of the game makes (`NotBuildable`) and objects of a kind the game does not have, dropped items and chests with more in a stack than an item can have, pieces in the wrong biome or in a dungeon, plants put down in groups (and says who made it). It does not look for field overrides, the cheating mark, forged ids, pieces in wards or far from their player: those need the player. `anticheat revert` takes away only what no tool of the game makes (if `NotBuildable` is `On`) and dropped items above their maximum (if `Items` is `On`; a dropped item has no owner on it, so they go whoever dropped them), and never the contents of a chest, a piece in the wrong biome or in a dungeon (the world may have been built under another rule then), an object the game does not have, or a piece of an admin; a piece whose maker the character guard does not know is left alone unless `anticheat revert all` is said (the creator is what the player's game wrote, and without a record there is no telling an admin's from a stranger's). It ignores `Action`, it cannot be undone, and `scan` first shows exactly what `revert` would take. `anticheat` alone says what has been found since the start. On a copy of the live world this was written for, a scan of 600,000 objects takes some tens of milliseconds and found exactly one thing no tool of the game makes -- a thistle -- and no unknown object, nothing in the wrong biome, no stack above its maximum.

## What it cannot see, and how it can be got round

Anything the game does inside the player's own game -- how fast a character runs, how hard a weapon hits, what a tool or a recipe costs, how much can be carried, how long a pickaxe reaches (the stamina of a character is in the character object, and `PaidPlanting` reads it for planting, nothing else). A cheat that is careful -- single pieces, within reach, on cultivated ground, with a mod that only turns them -- cannot be told from a player who does not use it. A player's inventory is not on the server, so nothing here can say that a player planted seeds they did not have (`PaidPlanting` checks the stamina a planting takes, not the seeds). An object is judged once, 1.2 s after it arrived: a game that sends an innocent object and rewrites it later is not seen again, and an object whose data never comes is counted and dropped. A game decides what character it calls its own; the rules that read the character (`Fly`, `Teleport`, `Speed`, `Creator`, `Distance`, `Identity`) trust it as far as it is checked (it must be made by the connection's own game), and a game that has none is held to that for pieces. The server accepts a write to any object from any game that sends a newer revision -- the vanilla server does -- so a modified game can still change another player's objects; the anti-cheat acts on a game that writes to a character, and leaves a character that has been written to out of its rules. Thousands of objects at once are not all looked at (`Flood`). And a kick is not a ban.
